# Proposal: Two-Day Engineering Offsite

## Summary

We propose a two-day offsite for the platform team in late November. The main reason is that over the last two quarters the team has grown from six to eleven people, and many of the newer members have never worked closely with each other outside of code review, which has made planning meetings slower and has led to a number of misunderstandings about ownership that we think could be resolved much more quickly in person than through more documents and more meetings.

## Goals

- Agree on ownership for the five services that currently have no clear owner.
- Draft the roadmap for the first half of next year.
- Give newer team members time to work directly with the people they depend on.
- Decide whether to keep the weekly sync or replace it with written updates.

## Agenda

### Day one

The morning starts with a short review of the past six months. Each service lead presents what went well, what did not go well, and what they would change. In the afternoon we split into three groups, one per product area, and each group drafts a list of the most important problems to solve next year, which we then bring back together at the end of the day to compare and merge into a single list.

### Day two

The second day is about decisions. We take the merged list from day one, estimate rough effort for each item, and pick the work for the first half of the year. After lunch we assign owners to the unowned services and agree on how on-call rotations will work for them. We finish early so people can travel home.

## Risks

It is possible that two days will not be enough to agree on ownership for every service, especially the billing service, which has historically been shared between three teams and has a long history of incidents that nobody wants to take responsibility for. If we do not reach agreement, we will write down the open questions and resolve them in the following two weeks.
